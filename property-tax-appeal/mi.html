📋 How to Appeal Property Taxes in Michigan

  1. Review Your Assessment Notice — Check the assessed value on your Michigan property tax notice. Compare it to recent sales of similar homes in your area.
  2. Research Comparable Sales — Find 3-5 recent sales of similar properties within 0.5 miles. Focus on homes with similar size, age, condition, and features.
  3. Document Property Issues — Note any problems that reduce your home's value: needed repairs, environmental issues, noise, easements, or functional obsolescence.
  4. File Your Appeal by the Deadline — In Michigan, you must file March Board of Review meeting. Contact your county assessor's office for exact procedures.
  5. Present Your Case — Attend the hearing with your evidence: comparable sales data, photos of property issues, independent appraisal if available.
  6. Consider Further Appeal — If denied, Michigan allows further appeal to a higher board or court.

❓ Michigan Property Tax Appeal FAQ

What is the property tax rate in Michigan?

The average effective property tax rate in Michigan is 1.54%. On a home valued at $218,100, that's approximately $3,359 per year. Rates vary by county and municipality.

When is the deadline to appeal property taxes in Michigan?

In Michigan, the appeal deadline is March Board of Review meeting. Check with your local assessor's office for exact dates, as they can vary by county.

How often are properties reassessed in Michigan?

Michigan reassessment cycles vary by jurisdiction — typically every 1-5 years.

Do I need a lawyer to appeal in Michigan?

No, you can appeal on your own. However, for high-value properties or complex cases, a property tax attorney or consultant may increase your chances. Many work on contingency (typically 30-50% of first year's savings).

What evidence do I need for a Michigan property tax appeal?

The strongest evidence includes: comparable sales (3-5 recent sales of similar homes), an independent appraisal, photos of property defects, and documentation of any issues affecting value (noise, flooding, environmental contamination).

What are the chances of winning a property tax appeal in Michigan?

In Michigan, approximately 52% of property tax appeals result in a reduction. Nationally, success rates range from 30-60% depending on the jurisdiction and evidence quality.
