☀️ Solar Energy in North Dakota — What You Need to Know

Solar Potential

North Dakota averages 4.5 peak sun hours per day, providing good conditions for residential solar installations. A typical 6kW residential system in North Dakota produces approximately 7,884 kWh per year.

Electricity Costs

The average electricity rate in North Dakota is $0.118/kWh, below the national average, which extends payback periods but solar still provides long-term savings. With electricity prices rising 3-5% annually, locking in solar savings now protects against future rate increases.

Incentives & Credits

  • Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC): 30% of system cost through 2032, stepping down to 26% in 2033 and 22% in 2034
  • State Incentives: Check with your local utility and state energy office for current rebates and incentives
  • Net Metering (Yes): Full retail rate credit for excess energy sent to the grid
  • Property Tax Exemption: Many North Dakota counties exempt solar installations from property tax increases

ROI Analysis

Based on current rates and incentives, a typical solar installation in North Dakota pays for itself in approximately 16.6 years. Over a 25-year panel warranty period, homeowners can expect a 51% return on investment, with total savings of approximately $7,858 after accounting for system costs and incentives.

Installation Tips for North Dakota

  • Get at least 3 quotes from local installers — prices vary significantly
  • Check if your roof faces south or southwest for optimal production
  • Consider tree shading — even partial shade reduces output significantly
  • Ask about panel-level monitoring (microinverters or optimizers)
  • Verify your installer is NABCEP certified
  • Review your utility's interconnection process before signing
